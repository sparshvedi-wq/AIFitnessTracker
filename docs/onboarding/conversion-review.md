# Tally onboarding review

## What makes this worth finishing?

A visitor who tapped an ad about training around a changing schedule should get to try that promise on the first screen. The clearest reason to consider membership is the workout that visitor could actually start today. Those two moments now bookend the questionnaire.

## What the reference apps show

**Cal AI** introduces meal scanning, asks about habits and goals, and shows an editable personalized plan before the subscription decision in the captured iOS flow. The person sees a recognizable input, a result, and what the paid product does with it. For Tally, a time selector and a workout generated by the planner make that relationship tangible before asking for personal details. [Cal AI’s App Store description](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/cal-ai-calorie-tracker/id6480417616) · [Cal AI’s captured iOS sequence](https://design.lazyweb.com/company/cal-ai/app-tree)

**Flo** documents how the goal selected at setup changes both the content and interface. Its help page shows different experiences for tracking a cycle and following a pregnancy. Tally applies the same principle to sporting commitments, training locations and obstacles. Selecting tennis earns a tennis warm-up. Selecting a changing schedule earns an example of how unfinished work can fit into a later session. [Flo’s onboarding guide and interface examples](https://help.flo.health/hc/en-us/articles/4406826484500-Setting-up-your-Flo-account)

Those sources document product design. They supply no controlled estimate of the effect these choices would have on Tally purchases. The judgments below are design decisions grounded in the observed screens.

## Walkthrough

| Moment | What the user needs to understand | Decision in Tally |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First open | Does this solve a problem I actually have? | One working sample replaces a five-panel introduction. Change between 15, 30 and 45 minutes and the planner returns the exercise count and opening move. The sample leaves saved preferences alone. Membership and the yearly trial are visible at the first action. |
| Goal and age | Will it make sense for someone like me? | Ask for the goal first. Require a deliberate age-band selection before the physique question. If a teen picked an adult-only goal, bring them back to choose an available goal. |
| Goal preview and build preference | What does my goal change? | The mannequin highlights muscles from a sample plan. The build preference remains skippable. The app makes no promise of achieving a pictured physique. |
| Experience and rhythm | Can I start here? Do I need fixed days? | Keep plain-language experience choices and the flexible weekly option. Remove the extrapolated annual target and the first mark that used to appear before a real session. |
| Obstacles and variety | Did you listen to my answer? | A returner sees what happens after a break. A person unsure about technique sees an exercise guide. Someone bored with the same exercises sees a swap. The time and schedule examples appear once their workout context is known. |
| Name and gender | How much do I need to disclose? | The name stays optional. Gender has a “Prefer not to say” choice. The first-session preview uses the person's name once when supplied. |
| Sports and priorities | Does my sport actually change anything? | The sport, priority and warm-up screens appear on the sports branch. Changing the warm-up length shows its drills and durations. Long drill names wrap on small phones. |
| Location, home equipment and movement | Can I do what you recommend here? | Ask about dumbbell sizes only when relevant. The location demo opens on the selected setting. The visitor controls when to switch locations and can compare the planner’s exercises. |
| Time, energy, soreness and recent training | What happens on a difficult day? | Show how the proposed work can change. Mark the recovery chart as an estimate. The gym weight-feedback demo is reserved for relevant gym goals. Continue is available during story animations. |
| Photos and manual details | What am I giving permission for? | Show the three photo views and explain who receives an AI review. Offer manual details at the same decision. Height and weight remain visible, with optional muscle estimates and notes tucked away. |
| First session | What would I do if I joined today? | Show the actual starting assessment or adaptive workout, with illustrated moves, set prescriptions and a duration. Two movements introduce the session. The rest and the answer editor are one tap away. Short-session users get their time budget by default and can choose the longer assessment. |
| Membership | What do I get and when do I pay? | Go straight from that workout to one offer screen. A single benefit reflects the visitor’s answers. Show the price per billing period, trial eligibility, renewal and CTA together. Canceling the preview returns to pricing. Looking at a welcome offer is a separate choice. |

## How much onboarding remains

The default route has 24 top-level screens. A home dumbbell user choosing “Short on time” and manual entry has 26. A gym strength user who also trains at home, plays tennis, and selects both time and schedule obstacles has 32. The photo camera and review can contain additional interactions. Answer editing, disclosure panels and the preview checkout are also outside those counts.

## Verification and first launch

The mobile browser walkthrough used a fresh synthetic profile, set a 15-minute session, chose manual entry, reached the first workout, reloaded on review and completed the annual preview. Separate checks covered the teen goal correction, old paywall drafts, monthly pricing, annual pay-now pricing, cancellation, and the first-year renewal wording on the welcome offer. Screen checks included a 320-pixel viewport, dark mode and reduced motion. These checks exercise the UI and saved state. Store billing and purchases require store sandbox verification.

Launch this as one baseline. For the first cohort, count onboarding starts, first-session previews reached, trial starts, first workouts logged and paid renewals after trial. Compare people from the same acquisition source and allow each trial to mature before reading its paid result. If many people reach pricing but few start a trial, investigate the offer and checkout. If people start trials but never log a workout, inspect the transition into the first session. These counts can be collected without names, photos, body measurements or free text.
